A people-powered campaign against invasive species

Invasive species spread one backyard at a time. So does the response.

Lantern Hunt is a free iPhone app that helps people find, remove, and record spotted lanternflies wherever they live. It is the first step toward a platform that connects the people removing invasive species with the agencies, landowners, and sponsors working to stop them.

The first target · Lycorma delicatula

The spotted lanternfly is spreading. The people who see it first can help.

The spotted lanternfly was first detected in the United States in Pennsylvania in 2014. USDA APHIS now reports some degree of infestation in 19 states and the District of Columbia. It feeds on grapevines, hops, stone fruit trees, and hardwoods, and the sticky fluid it leaves behind feeds sooty mold, a black fungal coating that blocks sunlight and keeps the plants beneath from photosynthesizing.

It is also easy to spot once you know what to look for. Adults and late-stage nymphs are hard to miss, and egg masses sit on exposed surfaces such as bark, stone, and outdoor equipment. That makes it a practical first species for people who want to help.

Live on the App Store

A field tool for finding, removing, and documenting lanternflies.

  1. Learn to identify

    Identification guides for adults, nymphs, and egg masses, with photos and the features to check.

  2. Find and remove

    When you find lanternflies or an egg mass, remove them and record what you found and how many.

  3. Document it

    Take a before and an after photo. The app records the location of the removal from your phone.

  4. Get it verified

    A person reviews every submission. Approved removals earn points and appear on the community map.

  5. Earn cash rewards

    As sponsors fund bounty programs, verified removals will earn money for the species and places those programs target.

Reviewed and private

Every removal is reviewed before it counts.

Each submission carries a before and an after photograph, and a person approves or rejects it. That review is what makes the totals on this site reliable and what would let a sponsor pay for results. Participants’ exact locations are visible only to the reviewer, and the app has no third-party tracking software.

One platform

Individual acts of conservation, turned into one coordinated effort.

Lantern Hunt brings together five things that usually live in separate programs. Four of them are working in the app today. The fifth, financial incentives, is the next stage.

Operating

People

Anyone with an iPhone can join. Accounts require no name or email address.

Operating

Operating

Photographic verification

Geographic data

Proposal

Financial incentives

Partner-funded rewards that vary by species, location, and season, so funders pay most for the removals they care about most.

Together the first four have produced 2,000+ verified spotted lanternfly kills across 6 states, as of October 17, 2026.

A long record

People have been paid to remove harmful species for nearly 150 years.

  • Minnesota, 1877

    The state paid farmers up to a dollar a bushel for grasshoppers and 50 cents a gallon for their eggs during a locust plague.

  • Louisiana, since 2002

    Trappers earn $6 for every nutria tail, and nutria damage to coastal marsh has fallen by more than 90 percent.

  • Florida, since 2017

    Contractors are paid by the hour and by the foot for Burmese pythons and have removed more than 11,000.

  • Japan, 1993–2024

    Bounty trappers and then a professional team removed more than 32,000 mongooses, and Amami-Ōshima was declared mongoose-free.

Where this goes

From one species to a national response network.

The Lantern Hunt app is the foundation. The plan is a platform where ordinary people anywhere can take part, have their removals verified, and be connected with the organizations that need invasive species removed and are willing to pay for it. These pieces are in development.
