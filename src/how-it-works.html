How it works

Spot it. Remove it. Record it.

Lantern Hunt is a free iPhone app for finding, removing, and recording spotted lanternflies. This page walks through it, from learning what to look for to an approved removal on the map.

Identification

Know what you’re looking for.

The app records two kinds of removal, adults and egg masses. Nymphs are worth knowing too. Black nymphs appear in late spring, and red ones mean adults are only weeks away.

From sighting to reward

Five steps, one record.

  1. Choose what you found

    Start a hunt and pick adult lanternflies or an egg mass, then enter how many you removed.

  2. Photograph before and after

    One photo shows what you found. The second shows it removed. The app saves the time each photo was taken.

  3. Submit with location

    The app attaches the removal’s location from your phone’s location services, with its GPS accuracy estimate.

  4. Wait for review

    A reviewer approves or rejects each submission. Approved removals earn points and appear on the in-app community map. How review works

  5. Earn a cash reward

    As Lantern Hunt launches sponsor-funded bounty programs, approved removals will also earn cash, with amounts set by each sponsor. The current app awards points. How sponsorship works

Proposal

Sponsors can pay people to remove invasive species.

Lantern Hunt already has ordinary people finding, removing, photographing, and submitting an invasive species, and every submission is reviewed. The next step is to let sponsors pay for that work. Government agencies, conservation organizations, agricultural businesses, and other funders could offer cash rewards for verified removals, set by species, area, season, or conservation priority.

  1. 01

    People remove

    Ordinary people find and remove a harmful invasive species, just as they do in the app today.

  2. 02

    They submit proof

    Each removal goes in with a before and an after photo, the quantity, and the location.

  3. 03

    Lantern Hunt verifies

    A reviewer approves or rejects every submission. Only approved removals would be paid.

  4. 04

    Sponsors pay for results

    A sponsor funds rewards for the species and places it chooses and pays only for verified removals.

Why fund removal this way

Pay only for results

Money goes to removals that have been photographed and approved.

Direct effort where it counts

Higher rewards for a priority species, a vulnerable watershed, a vineyard edge, or egg-mass season draw people to the removals that do the most good.

Reach far more people

A reward open to anyone with a phone can put far more hands to work than a crew of staff or contractors.

See what the money did

Sponsors receive a report and map of the verified removals their funding paid for.

Rewards

Paying people for the removals that matter most.

A bounty doesn’t have to pay the same for every removal. Sponsored programs can pay more for the ones that do the most good: a newly arrived species, a priority watershed or vineyard edge, the weeks before eggs hatch, or an egg mass instead of a single adult. Higher rewards draw people’s effort to exactly those places and moments.

An egg mass holds 30 to 50 eggs, so destroying one before it hatches prevents far more lanternflies than removing a single adult.

The app’s points already follow the same logic.

In the field

Hunt responsibly.

Lantern Hunt is one part of a much larger effort led by state and federal programs. Their guidance comes first.

  • Follow your state or local authorities’ guidance on spotted lanternflies, including any quarantine rules about moving vehicles, firewood, and outdoor items.
  • Report sightings through your state’s official channel as well. USDA APHIS asks people who find spotted lanternflies to report them to their state. Find your state contact.
  • Search only on property where you have permission to be.
  • Watch for traffic, uneven ground, and other hazards, and take care around trees and ladders.
  • Kill only insects you have identified as spotted lanternflies. Check the guide above if you are unsure.
