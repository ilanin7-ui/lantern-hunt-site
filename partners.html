Partnerships

Put people-powered removal to work where you need it.

People are already using Lantern Hunt to find and remove spotted lanternflies in six states. Partners can direct that kind of effort toward the species, places, and seasons that matter to them. Anything marked Proposal is a plan we want to build with partners, not a program that exists today.

Proposal

How a sponsored incentive would work.

Today, participants earn points for verified removals. The proposal is to let a partner add a cash reward. A county parks department, for example, could offer a set payment for each verified spotted lanternfly egg mass destroyed in its parks between November and April.

  1. 01

    The partner sets the terms

    Which species, which area, which dates, and a total budget.

  2. 02

    Rates follow priorities

    The partner can pay more for the removals it cares about most, as described below.

  3. 03

    Participants hunt as usual

    They remove, photograph, and submit through the app. A reviewer approves or rejects each submission.

  4. 04

    Approved removals are paid

    Payments go only to approved removals inside the campaign’s terms, and the partner receives a report and map of the results.

What rates could depend on

Species

What is removed

Higher rates for the species a funder is targeting, or for one newly arrived in a region.

Life stage

When in its life

More for an egg mass than an adult, since one egg mass holds 30 to 50 eggs.

Location

Where it happens

Premiums inside priority areas, such as a vineyard edge, a park, a watershed, or the leading edge of an infestation.

Season

When it matters most

Rates that rise in the weeks when removal does the most good, such as egg-mass season before spring hatch.

Variable payments are an established approach. Florida pays its python contractors more for longer snakes and a separate amount for nests. More examples.

Two examples in detail

What a program could look like.

These two are worked out further than the others. They are proposals, with no agreements in place, and the same planning would apply to any of the six species.

Proposal

Vineyards and the spotted lanternfly

Grapevines are among the spotted lanternfly’s hosts, according to USDA APHIS, and grape growers are among the hardest hit. Vineyards in affected areas have a direct stake in removal on and around their land.

The proposal: work with vineyards to organize removal in the seasons that matter most, using Lantern Hunt to document the work, with the option of sponsor-funded incentives for participants.

What we would work out with a vineyard

  • Where removal would happen, and who may enter the property
  • How to target egg masses from fall through spring and adults from mid-summer
  • How results would be reported back, and how often

Sources: USDA APHIS; Penn State. A Pennsylvania vineyard, shown for illustration; not a Lantern Hunt partner. Photo credit

Proposal

European green crab in Washington State

In January 2022, Washington’s governor issued an emergency proclamation directing the Department of Fish and Wildlife to eradicate, reduce, or contain European green crab. WDFW reports roughly 1.74 million green crabs removed from Washington waters between January 2022 and March 2025.

The proposal: explore whether verified removal records could support existing trapping and monitoring, in coordination with WDFW, tribes, shellfish growers, and other permitted partners.

How we would approach it

  • Work within Washington’s rules on handling and trapping green crab, rather than as open public trapping
  • Start from what current removal programs need
  • Test whether verified records add value before proposing incentives
