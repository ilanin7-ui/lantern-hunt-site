Field guide

How to Kill Spotted Lanternflies

State agriculture officials across the Northeast give the same advice: if you see a spotted lanternfly, kill it.

Adults

Adults are about an inch long, with gray, black-spotted forewings and red hind wings that flash when they jump. They're slow to fly but quick to hop, so approach from above and behind and squash them by hand or underfoot. Adults are active from mid-summer until the first hard freezes.

Nymphs

Young nymphs, out from late spring, are small, black, and white-spotted. Older nymphs turn red with white spots. They hop away fast, so a quick swat or stomp works better than trying to grab them.

Egg masses

From fall through spring, the best thing you can do is destroy egg masses, gray mud-like patches holding 30 to 50 eggs each. Scrape them into a bag or container with rubbing alcohol, seal it, and throw it away. Full guide to finding and destroying egg masses

Don't move them

Lanternflies spread by hitching rides on cars, trailers, firewood, and outdoor equipment. Check your vehicle before you leave an infested area, especially in fall when egg masses are being laid.

Keep score with Lantern Hunt

Lantern Hunt is a free iPhone app built by a group of Yale students. Photograph each lanternfly or egg mass you remove, earn points, climb the leaderboard, and see removals add up on a shared community map.
