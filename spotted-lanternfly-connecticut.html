Connecticut

Spotted Lanternflies in Connecticut

Spotted lanternflies are now widespread in Connecticut, with the heaviest populations along the shoreline.

What the state says

The Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station (CAES) leads the state's response and has issued a quarantine to slow the spread. Its advice to residents is simple: if you see a spotted lanternfly, destroy it.

How to report them

CAES takes spotted lanternfly reports through its online reporting form rather than by phone. Egg masses can be reported the same way as sightings. Check the CAES website for the current form.

Fall is egg season

Adults die off with the cold, but before they do, females lay egg masses on trees, stone walls, fences, cars, and outdoor furniture. Each mass is 30 to 50 lanternflies next spring. Scrape them into a bag with rubbing alcohol, seal it, and toss it. How to find and destroy egg masses

Keep score with Lantern Hunt

Lantern Hunt is a free iPhone app built by a group of Yale students. Photograph each lanternfly or egg mass you remove, earn points, climb the leaderboard, and see removals add up on a shared community map.
